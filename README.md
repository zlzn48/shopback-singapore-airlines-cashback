## What Is Singapore Airlines Cashback, Really?

Cashback on Singapore Airlines isn't a loyalty programme run by the airline itself. It's a rebate paid to you by a third-party cashback platform — most commonly ShopBack — when you start your booking session on that platform, click through to singaporeair.com, and complete checkout in the same session. The platform earns an affiliate commission from Singapore Airlines for sending you there, and it shares a slice of that commission back with you in real money.

The key mental shift: you pay the exact same fare you'd pay booking directly. Cashback is funded out of Singapore Airlines' marketing budget, not subtracted from your ticket price. So a S$2,000 return fare to Europe at 0.5% cashback returns S$10 — not life-changing on its own, but it compounds across a year of travel, hotels, and everyday shopping if you make the platform your default starting point.

Singapore Airlines also runs its own KrisFlyer miles programme, which is separate from cashback. Miles earn you award flights and upgrades; cashback earns you SGD you can spend on anything. The two can stack — you still earn KrisFlyer miles on a ticket booked through a cashback portal, as long as you enter your KrisFlyer number at checkout.

## Singapore Airlines Cashback: What's Excluded

Singapore Airlines cashback does not apply to everything you might book on singaporeair.com. Based on the terms published on ShopBack's Singapore Airlines store page, the following are excluded:

- Gift card or voucher redemptions
- Taxes
- Service charges
- Booking fees
- Any rejected, cancelled, refunded, exchanged, or returned purchases

In practice, this means cashback applies to the base airfare component of your booking — the ticket price before taxes and surcharges. On a S$2,000 fare where S$400 is taxes and surcharges, cashback applies to the S$1,600 base fare. At 0.5%, that's S$8 back. Not huge, but free money for a 10-second click-through.

## Common Tracking Problems and How to Fix Them

Cashback fails more often than the platforms like to admit. The usual suspects, in rough order of frequency:

- **Ad blockers or privacy extensions** stripping the tracking cookie. Disable them for the ShopBack and singaporeair.com domains.
- **Incognito or private browsing** mode, which doesn't persist cookies. Always book in a normal browser window.
- **Switching devices mid-booking** — starting on the ShopBack app and finishing on desktop breaks the session. Pick one and stick with it.
- **Clicking another cashback site's link** after ShopBack. The last-click-wins model means whichever platform's cookie is most recent gets the attribution. Don't comparison-shop across cashback sites for the same transaction.
- **Applying an unapproved promo code.** If the code isn't listed on ShopBack's store page, there's a real risk the merchant rejects the attribution.
- **Payment failure and retry.** If your card declines and you retry, restart the session from ShopBack rather than refreshing the singaporeair.com checkout page.

If cashback doesn't track within 48 hours, ShopBack's help centre has a missing cashback claim form. You'll need your booking reference, the date and time of purchase, and the order amount. Claims are usually resolved within a few weeks, though travel claims can take longer since the merchant only confirms after travel.

## Singapore Airlines Cashback vs KrisFlyer Miles — Do They Conflict?

A common confusion: do you have to choose between cashback and KrisFlyer miles? No. They're separate programmes running on separate rails.

- **KrisFlyer miles** are earned by entering your KrisFlyer number at checkout. They accrue based on fare class and distance flown, and can be redeemed for award flights, upgrades, and partner rewards.
- **ShopBack cashback** is earned by clicking through from ShopBack before booking. It accrues as a percentage of the base fare and pays out in SGD.

Both apply to the same ticket. Enter your KrisFlyer number during booking, and you earn miles on the flight while ShopBack tracks the cashback on the transaction. The only overlap to watch for: if you redeem KrisFlyer miles for an award ticket (paying S$0 in cash), there's no base fare for cashback to apply to — so cashback only makes sense on paid fares, not award bookings.

For paid flights, the stack looks like this: ShopBack cashback on the base fare + KrisFlyer miles on the flight + your credit card's miles or cashback on the total spend + any promo code discount. On a S$2,000 fare, that could be S$10 cashback, 4,000+ KrisFlyer miles (depending on fare class), and another 2–4 miles per dollar from a travel credit card. None of these cancel each other out.
